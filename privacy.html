FrameForge

Privacy policy

Last updated 8 November 2026

FrameForge is a desktop app that runs on your own computer. Its My Channel section can connect to a YouTube channel you own. This page explains what it reads, where that goes and how to remove it.

What FrameForge reads

FrameForge asks Google for two read-only permissions: to view your YouTube account, and to view its YouTube Analytics reports. With them it reads:

  • your channel's name, handle, picture and its subscriber, view and video counts;
  • each upload's title, description, thumbnail, length, publish date and privacy status;
  • each upload's views, likes, comments, shares, watch time, average view duration and percentage, subscribers gained, and audience retention.

FrameForge cannot upload, change or delete anything on YouTube. It never sees your Google password, because you sign in on Google's own page.

How it is used

The numbers are shown to you in the app, compared with each other and with the edits of videos you made in FrameForge, and used to suggest how to edit your next videos.

Where it is kept

Everything stays on your computer. The numbers are kept in FrameForge's local database, and thumbnails in its data folder. The lasting sign-in Google provides is kept in Windows Credential Manager.

Who it is shared with

Nobody. FrameForge has no server of its own, so this data is not sent to its makers. It is not sold, not used for advertising, and not sent to any AI service. FrameForge connects only to Google and YouTube to read it.

How long it is kept, and how to delete it

It is kept until you remove it. Disconnect in My Channel ends the sign-in and deletes everything FrameForge read from your channel. If Google has already ended the sign-in, connect again first, then disconnect.

You can also remove FrameForge's access at any time from your Google account permissions.

YouTube and Google

FrameForge uses YouTube API Services. By connecting a channel you also agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google's handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.
